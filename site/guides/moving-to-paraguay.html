Why people look at Paraguay

People considering Paraguay often care about cost of living, climate, location in South America, and a slower pace of life. This guide does not rank countries or publish "best of" lists. Your own priorities matter more than any ranking.

Your reasons matter more than anyone else's list. Write down what you need for work, family, health, and money before you lock in flights.

Use this guide as a planning map. Ask questions in the free ParaguayNext community when you want peer context.

How to use this section safely

  • Treat social media summaries about "easy residency" as marketing until a licensed professional confirms what applies to you.
  • Do not book irreversible moves (selling a home, quitting a job, shipping everything) until your own professional has confirmed your plan.
  • Keep a simple folder (digital and paper) for contracts, tickets, and receipts you already have.

Planning your move in practice

Before you travel

  • Decide what "good enough" looks like for work, family, health, and money.
  • Talk to a licensed professional in Paraguay about legal and tax steps that apply to you. Details on this are coming later. For now, check with a licensed professional in Paraguay.
  • Plan flexible lodging for the first weeks. Avoid long leases until you know the neighborhood.
  • Set up money access that does not depend on a single card (see Guide 2 banking basics for product facts that are already sourced).
  • Keep backup copies of identity documents you already travel with. Path-specific document lists are not published in this draft.

After you arrive

  • Follow Guide 2 for first-month logistics (phone, housing habits, banking product basics, meeting people).
  • Details on local ID, tax registration, and immigration appointments are coming later. For now, check with a licensed professional in Paraguay.

Timeline shape, budget thinking, and common mistakes

Suggested planning phases (structure only)

  1. Research: Read peer guides, talk to a licensed professional, join the free community, and decide if Paraguay fits your life.
  2. Personal paperwork plan: Build your own list with a professional. Official category lists and fees are not restated here.
  3. Money setup: Open or keep a reliable international account for ATM cash and USD storage if you qualify (Guide 2). Keep a plan for a local Paraguayan bank after arrival.
  4. Travel and lodging: Book flexible lodging for the first weeks.
  5. After arrival: Phone, banking, housing, and meeting people (Guide 2). Legal ID and tax steps: check with a licensed professional in Paraguay.

These phases are a personal planning shape, not a government timeline.

Budget thinking (no invented numbers)

Plan for:

  • Flights and excess baggage or shipping
  • Temporary lodging
  • Local transport and deposits
  • Professional fees (lawyer, translator, accountant) if you use them
  • A cash buffer for delays

Details on official immigration fees and sample monthly living budgets are coming later. For now, check with a licensed professional in Paraguay for fee questions.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Treating social media summaries as official rules
  • Arriving with only one payment card and no backup
  • Signing a long housing contract before you understand the area
  • Expecting ParaguayNext or any free community to replace a licensed lawyer or accountant
